# SoulOS UX review (v3 → v4)

*SOUL M · round AMOLED, 466 × 466 logical px (the layout scales 1:1.03 to a 480 × 480 panel) · 2 Oct 2026*

This review checks the SoulOS web prototype (`os/index.html`, mirrored to `site/os.html`) against what works on small, round, one-handed screens, lists what was wrong in v3, and records what v4 changes. Everything listed under "Changes" is implemented and covered by `os/tests/soulos.test.mjs`.

## 1. What good looks like on a tiny round screen

Distilled from the watchOS and Wear OS design guidance, the Pebble and Tamagotchi lineage, and the public post-mortems of the Humane AI Pin and Rabbit R1 (2024 reviews):

| Principle | Where it comes from | What it means for SOUL |
|---|---|---|
| **Glance in 2 s, act in 5 s** | watchOS "glanceable" interactions; Wear OS tiles | Every screen says one thing. The top tasks finish in one or two gestures. |
| **The character is the interface** | Tamagotchi, Pebble's watchfaces, Anki Vector | State is shown by the eyes first, then by words. Nobody reads a status bar on a 44 mm disc. |
| **Never leave the user guessing** | Humane and Rabbit reviews: slow answers with no visible state, unclear whether the device heard you | Listening, thinking, talking, done and failed must look different, instantly, and errors must say what to do. |
| **Latency kills trust** | Same reviews; Doherty threshold (≈ 400 ms) | Feedback in < 100 ms (press, haptic, eyes), navigation in ≤ 250 ms, a "thinking" face the moment you let go. |
| **Use the edge** | Fitts's law on round displays; Wear OS curved text | The rim is the easiest target and the best place for words, dials and status. Corners do not exist. |
| **One back, everywhere** | Wear OS swipe-to-dismiss, watchOS crown | The same gesture (and the button) always goes up one level. |
| **Large targets, real contrast** | Apple 44 pt / Material 48 dp; WCAG 2.2 AA | ≥ 46 logical px per target (≈ 7 mm on the M glass), dim text ≥ 4.5:1, a large-text setting. |
| **Not a phone** | Humane's own pitch, and why it failed | No grids of icons, no cards, no settings jungle. Voice is optional, never the only way. |

## 2. Audit of v3

Severity: **H** = blocks a top task or misleads, **M** = slows it down, **L** = polish.

| # | Finding | Sev |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | The eyes were the old Face.cpp port: one cream colour, no designs, no birth roll. The founder-approved engine (`eyes/eyes.js`, 31 expressions, 120 designs) was not used. | H |
| 2 | Every AI answer was scripted. Claude mode only looked real; the page said so in small print. | H |
| 3 | Approving a Claude request took a tap on the notification, a screen change and a 1.2 s hold: three steps for the most time-critical task. | H |
| 4 | Setting an alarm took + → drag hours → drag minutes → ✓ → Set (5 touches) before anything was saved. | M |
| 5 | Errors were not modelled at all: no "offline", "bad key", "not allowed". | H |
| 6 | Thinking and Claude-working used the same dots; "needs you" was only an amber ring. The eyes did not distinguish listening, thinking, working, waiting and done. | M |
| 7 | No first-boot story: the device just started on the face. The "hold the glass" gesture, the core of the product, was never taught. | M |
| 8 | Back was inconsistent: the side button jumped home from a sub-screen (Settings › AI → home), while swipe ↓ went up one level. | M |
| 9 | Transitions ran 350–750 ms (card springs ≈ 0.35 s, notification slide 0.75 s, mask 0.45 s). Delightful, but slower than a glance. | M |
| 10 | Faint text at 30 % cream on black (≈ 2.6:1) was used for real labels (toggle states, "sending…"); no large-text option. | M |
| 11 | "Your API key" mode had nowhere to put a key. | M |
| 12 | Screen readers got nothing when the state changed (only notifications were live). | L |
| 13 | The page chrome had no designed focus, pressed or disabled states for its controls. | L |

## 3. Changes in v4

### The eyes are the UI
* The SOUL eyes engine v2.1 is **inlined** (between `SOUL-EYES` markers, regenerated by `os/tools/inline_eyes.py`), with all 120 designs. The old renderer is gone; the OS keeps only its layout springs (the eyes fill the face, or sit small above a screen) and the rim rings.
* Each unit is **born** with one design, rolled from a simulated chip id exactly as the firmware will roll it from the eFuse MAC (`rollFromChipId`).
* **Every state is said by the eyes first:** listening → `listening` + ice rim · thinking → `thinking` + an amber comet on the rim · Claude working → `working` (the reading scan) · Claude needs you → `shocked`, then wide `surprised` eyes looking at you + amber rim · done → `approve` (the green tick) · error → `confused` · notification → a glance to the rim where it lands · charging → `charging` (fills with the real level) · low battery → `hungry` · asleep → `sleepy`.
* Settings › **My SOUL** shows this unit's design: name, rarity (in its colour), number out of 120, odds ("1 in 133") and the chip id, plus **Rebirth (demo)**, which rolls a new chip and swaps the eyes during a blink.

### Claude works for real
* **Claude mode** uses the claude.ai `sample` capability: in the published artifact the viewer's own Claude answers (one consent prompt, then streaming). The eyes think until the first words stream in, then talk.
* **Your API key** calls `api.anthropic.com/v1/messages` straight from the page (`claude-opus-5-5`, effort `low`, `anthropic-dangerous-direct-browser-access: true`, server-side fallback). The key is pasted on the round keyboard (shown masked) or in the panel; it stays in memory, or in this browser's storage only if "Remember on this device" is ticked. **ChatGPT** works the same way with an OpenAI key.
* **Tool-like actions** through a small JSON protocol: `{"say", "face"?, "actions": [...]}` with five actions (`alarm.set`, `timer.start`, `reminder.create`, `note.create`, `focus.start`). Validation is strict: at most three actions, exact keys, `HH:MM` 24 h times, numeric ranges, enums; anything else is dropped and shown as "N actions ignored". The device state (time, alarms, reminders, timer, notes) is sent with every question so Claude can answer "what's next?".
* **No AI** keeps the on-device rules (times, alarms, reminders, timers; the rest is kept in Notes).
* **Errors** are shown by the eyes (`confused`) plus one short line that says what to do: not connected here, not allowed, too many questions, offline, key rejected, no key, declined. When the AI is unreachable, the on-device rules still do what they can (an alarm, a reminder, a timer).
* **Voice:** holding the glass uses the Web Speech API where the browser allows a microphone; in the claude.ai frame (no microphone) a demo phrase is "heard" instead, and the rim says so. Notes dictated on the Notes screen are saved on the device with no AI round trip.

### Fewer steps for the top tasks

| Task | v3 | v4 |
|---|---|---|
| Set an alarm | 5 touches (+, hours, minutes, ✓, Set) | **2 gestures** by voice: hold the glass and say "wake me at 6:30", let go. **3 touches** by hand: +, drag the hour, ✓ (saved at once; tap it later for days and a label). |
| Write a note | Notes → + → type → ✓ | the same, or on Notes: hold, speak, let go |
| Ask Claude | hold (scripted) or type | hold and speak, or type; a real answer |
| Approve a Claude request | tap the notification → hold 1.2 s on the Claude screen | **hold the glass** wherever the amber ring shows (face, Today, the notification itself); double-tap = deny |

### Navigation, motion, feedback
* **One back:** the side button and swipe ↓ (or →) always go up one level (Settings › AI → Settings → home). On the keyboard, pulling the text field down closes it and keeps the draft. During first boot, swipe ↓ is "previous step" and the side button skips.
* **Motion budget 250 ms:** card springs re-tuned (k 560, c 40: lands in ≈ 200 ms with ≤ 6 % overshoot), notification slide 250 ms, the orbit opens in ≈ 270 ms, fades ≤ 220 ms. Reduced-motion users get instant changes.
* **Haptics and sound** (simulated: the drawn device ticks, phones vibrate; WebAudio "hums"): a tick on press, a double on done, a heavy buzz for "needs you", a falling hum for errors.

### First boot
Birth (the chip id types out, then the eyes open with `hello`; rarer designs get `excited`) → name (one tap on a suggestion, or type) → brain (Claude / your key / ChatGPT / No AI, each with its live status) → **hold the glass** (the rim pulses ice; holding turns the eyes to `listening`; letting go gives `approve`). Four steps, about 15 s. It runs once (remembered), again from the panel ("↺ First boot") or with `#boot`.

### Accessibility
* Dim text raised to 62 % and faint to 42 % cream (≥ 4.5:1 on black for dim); **Settings › Text size: large** steps every type size up one notch and brightens dim text further.
* A live region announces what the eyes show (listening, thinking, Claude needs you, errors).
* Panel controls have visible focus rings, hover and pressed states, disabled states, and an error state on key fields.

### Page chrome (the founder's design brief)
Direction: **"Orbit, quiet frame"**: the device is the hero, the page is a calm instrument panel around it. Tokens at the top of the CSS: type scale 12/14/16/20/28/40, spacing 4–32, radii 6/10/pill, motion 120/200/250 ms, one accent per theme (SOUL amber: `#9A5A00` light, `#FFB347` dark) on warm-grey neutrals. The "Your AI" section now has a status line with one state colour (live / needs a key / not connected), the key fields only when they matter, and a "Remember on this device" choice.

## 4. Still open
* The demo cannot hear a real microphone inside claude.ai (the frame refuses it) and cannot reach api.anthropic.com from there (network blocked); those two paths work when `site/os.html` is opened directly.
* Large text is a fixed step, not continuous; long Claude answers (> 200 characters) shrink to 20 px rather than scroll.
* Rarity colours (legendary gold on black) are decorative, not used for meaning.
